# Mountain Whisper — Voice & Copy Guide

The brand speaks like a thoughtful person writing at dawn, not a marketing team. Contemplative, specific, a little literary. The reader is a founder, executive, or reflective traveler — assume intelligence and exhaustion.

## Voice in one line

*Lyrical, spare, grounded in the physical Himalayas — never selling, always inviting.*

## Reference samples (canonical — match this register)

- "Where silence speaks." — hero tagline
- "In the Himalayas, silence is not empty — it's full. Full of clarity, perspective, and the kind of stillness that allows you to hear what truly matters."
- "Clarity doesn't come from doing more — it comes from creating space."
- "The Mountain Whisper exists for others who care deeply about their work but want to work with intention."

## Writing rules

1. **Lead with the concrete, not the abstract.** "Pine smoke at 4,200m" beats "an immersive sensory experience."
2. **Short sentences, then one long one.** Rhythm matters. Don't write in uniform paragraphs.
3. **Em-dashes and semicolons are welcome.** So are single-sentence paragraphs. Avoid bullet-point-ification of prose.
4. **No superlatives, no hype.** Never "best," "world-class," "premium," "transformative," "curated experiences" (ironic — the brand *is* curated, so show it; don't say it).
5. **No second-person sales pressure.** Prefer "those who…" or "for people who…" over "you will discover." Invite, don't push.
6. **Name real places, seasons, altitudes, hours.** "Manang in autumn, 3,540m" is more honest than "a remote mountain village."
7. **CTAs are understated.** "Begin" / "Enquire" / "Read more" — never "Book now!", "Get started today", "Claim your spot".
8. **Honor silence on the page.** If a section could be three sentences, make it two.

## Banned phrases / AI-marketing tells

These flag a passage as generic:
- "unlock your potential", "elevate your experience", "journey of a lifetime"
- "nestled in the heart of…", "embark on…", "immerse yourself in…"
- "whether you're a X or a Y, …"
- "in today's fast-paced world"
- "we believe that…"
- "carefully curated" (just curate; don't announce it)
- "at Mountain Whisper, we…"
- "here's what sets us apart"
- Emoji-led bullet points (🌄 ✨ 🧘)
- Alliterative tripled adjectives ("serene, soulful, stirring")
- "It's not just X — it's Y." (reductive marketing cliché)

## Structural patterns that work

- **Title — fragment subtitle**: "Between Decisions — a pause for leaders carrying weight."
- **Body paragraph**: open with a concrete image or observation, close with a reflection.
- **Section endings**: often a short sentence standing alone. Like this.
- **Lists**: use them for practical info (included/excluded, itinerary). Never for emotional or philosophical content — prose only there.

## Tone by surface

- **Hero**: 3–6 words. Evocative, not descriptive.
- **Journey card subtitle**: one line, a specific hook — "Seven quiet days in Manang, at your own pace."
- **Journey description**: 2–4 paragraphs, opens with who it's for as a feeling, not a demographic.
- **Forms / UI microcopy**: plain, warm, unfussy. "We'll reply within two days." Not "Thanks! We've received your submission and will be in touch shortly."
- **Error messages**: quietly apologetic, specific. "That email doesn't look right — mind checking?" Not "Invalid input."
- **Emails (Resend templates)**: written as a human would, signed with a first name if possible.

## Length discipline

When asked to write copy, produce the shortest version that still has rhythm. Cut adverbs. Cut "really," "very," "truly." If a sentence works without a word, remove the word.

## Final check before shipping copy

Read it aloud. If it sounds like a LinkedIn post or a SaaS landing page, rewrite it. If it sounds like a letter from someone who has actually walked those trails, ship it.
